What the charts and scores mean

Food charts. Bars are what you ate; the yellow mark is your goal for that day. Protein, carbs and fat use the same colours as the food log.

Fitness score. My own formula, not a standard test. Strength is your best estimated 1RM in the last 120 days against your goals (squat 500, bench 365, deadlift 500, OHP 200). Running is half weekly mileage against 20 mi, half speed-per-heartbeat over the last 28 days. Use it as a trend line, not a number to compare with anyone else.

Projected race times. Riegel formula, based on your fastest run of the last 90 days. They are only as good as that run — if your log has no hard efforts, the projections will read slow.

Yesterday and averages. A dash means that day hasn't synced yet. The averages cover the last 7 and 30 days up to yesterday, using whatever days have data.

Where the body numbers come from. Bodyweight is whatever you typed in Body Log. Steps, HRV, sleep and resting heart rate come from intervals.icu, for finished days only.

Runs. They come from intervals.icu on their own, twice a day. There is nothing to log by hand.

Where the numbers come from

Every amount you log is your amount ÷ the label's serving size, times each number on the label. Logging 85 g of something with a 55 g serving counts 1.55 servings.

Labels are rounded by law, so "4 × carbs + 4 × protein + 9 × fat" rarely matches the printed calories exactly. The app checks this after a scan and only flags it when the gap is large enough to suggest a misread.

Recipes divide the sum of every ingredient by the number of servings you say it makes. When you log a recipe, the entry keeps how much of each ingredient was in your portion.

Each label read is a separate, paid call to the Anthropic API using your own key.
